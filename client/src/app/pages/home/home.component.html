01 - Purpose

Why Am I Making This?

I was making flashcards to study Design Patterns — alongside SOLID principles, language syntax, OOP keywords, REST, and APIs — when I thought: why not just build an accessible site I can actually refer back to?

It's not exactly flashcards, but the goal is the same: a living reference of examples I've written myself, across multiple languages. I've picked up a new language at every job I've had. Context switching causes memory fatigue; a good reference doesn't.

TLDR — studying through the craft of creation.

03 - Approach
01
Roll the Randomizer

In real engineering, we rarely get to pick the problems we face. We just have to solve them.

02
Read and Explore

The GoF chapter is the foundation, but we go wider than that. Medium posts, YouTube deep dives, real codebases. We want to actually understand the thing before touching any code.

03
Scope the Problem

Pick a concrete domain and a specific problem within it. The scope needs to be small enough to finish and real enough to matter, useable as a solid reference piece.

04
Find a Complement

Look for a second pattern that slots in naturally alongside the main one without becoming the focus. Because is software ever simple?

05
Build It

Once we have our idea, we want to create an architecture, throw ideas at the whiteboard, and start slapping the keyboard.

06
Write It Up

After the build, we put the page together. We cite our sources, talk about the intent, go through some of the notes taken on the pattern, and showcase the structure, participants, and patterns within the architecture. We highlight the strengths and weaknesses associated with the pattern, share what the experience was actually like, and some other fun stuff.
